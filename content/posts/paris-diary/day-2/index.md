## Day 2: The Left Bank (Sunday, 8 November)

### Another early start

I woke up early again without an alarm. I had gone to bed early after a long hot shower, and the sleep was just as good as the night before.

The plan for the day was the **Latin Quarter, the Luxembourg Gardens and Saint-Germain-des-Prés**.

### The Grande Mosquée de Paris

My first stop was the **Grande Mosquée de Paris**. I took **line 3, then line 7**. The mosque's style reminded me of the mosques back home, so it felt oddly familiar so far from Morocco.

Inside the prayer hall there were a few young men, and it was quiet and beautiful. I also explored the main courtyard and the gardens. It was a calm, spiritual morning, a good way to start a Sunday in a big city.

### Breakfast on rue Mouffetard

After the mosque I went looking for breakfast on **rue Mouffetard**, a street known for its food. I loved the atmosphere. It was lively, with food and vegetable shops, fresh bakeries and people shopping for their Sunday.

I walked the whole length of the street, then sat down for a coffee and a slice of **vanilla flan**, which was on my "to taste" list. I crossed it off.

### The Arènes de Lutèce

Next I visited the **Arènes de Lutèce**, a partly ruined Roman amphitheatre. In its round arena a group of Parisians were playing football. It was a nice thing to see: two thousand years of history, and today it's a neighbourhood pitch.

### Saint-Étienne-du-Mont

I was heading toward the **Panthéon**, but on the way I stopped at the **church of Saint-Étienne-du-Mont**. It was Sunday, and inside I found Christians from different countries at prayer. It was a quiet, respectful moment.

### Reading in the Luxembourg Gardens

I passed the **Sorbonne** and reached the **Jardin du Luxembourg**. I had been craving this: sitting in one of those famous green chairs with a book.

I picked the quietest corner I could find near the **Grand Bassin** and relaxed for a good 30 to 40 minutes.

{{< figure src="luxembourg.jpg" alt="Green chairs near the Grand Bassin in the Luxembourg Gardens" caption="My quiet corner near the Grand Bassin." >}}

### Boulevard Saint-Germain

Then I walked along **Boulevard Saint-Germain** and visited the church of **Saint-Germain-des-Prés**, inside and out.

I passed the famous cafés **Les Deux Magots** and **Café de Flore**, but another place on the other side interested me more: **Brasserie Lipp**. It was here, on **29 October 1965**, that the Moroccan left-wing leader **El Mehdi Ben Barka** was kidnapped. It was a strange feeling to stand in front of a place I had read about for years.

### A late lunch on rue de Buci

For a late lunch I walked to **rue de Buci**, a lively street full of typical Parisian cafés.

### Pont Neuf, Sainte-Chapelle and the lovers' locks

After lunch and some rest I walked to the **Pont Neuf**, the oldest standing bridge in Paris, and enjoyed the view. Then I strolled around **La Sainte-Chapelle**.

I went back to the **Pont des Arts**, famous for its lovers' locks. They have been removed for good, which is a shame for the romance and a relief for safety. Some people still manage to attach new ones to the lamp posts. :D

### Dinner back on rue de Buci

After dark I returned to **rue de Buci** for dinner and a **hot chocolate** in a warm café. After a day of walking in the November cold, that was exactly what I needed.

I took the metro home, had a hot shower and went to bed, getting ready for another busy day.
