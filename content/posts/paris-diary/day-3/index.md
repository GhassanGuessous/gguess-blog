*Paris, 6 to 11 November 2026. This is part of my Paris diary. Previous: [Day 2]({{< relref "/posts/paris-diary/day-2" >}}).*

## Day 3: The Louvre Day (Monday, 9 November)

### Rain forecast, so I came prepared

I woke up early again. The forecast said rain was likely, so I swapped to a different jacket and packed an umbrella.

I took **line 11, then line 1**, to **Place de la Concorde**. It was going to be a big day, because it included something I'd been waiting for: **the Louvre**.

### Place de la Concorde

I started my journey at the square itself. It has a dark history, since it was an execution place, and it is now home to the **Egyptian obelisk**. I spent some time looking at the hieroglyphs carved on each of its four faces.

### The Tuileries in fall colors

My Louvre slot was booked for **11 am**, so I had more than an hour to spare. I used it to explore the **Jardin des Tuileries**.

I walked slowly from the **Bassin Octogonal** to the **Grand Bassin Rond**, enjoying the fall leaves in every shade from green to yellow to deep red.

I bought a hot **cappuccino** at a small café there and settled into one of the green chairs facing the basin. It was a freezing morning, so cold that I couldn't even hold my book to read. Instead I warmed my hands on the cup, put them in my jacket pockets, and just relaxed there until **10:40**.

{{< figure src="tuileries.jpg" alt="Autumn trees around the basin in the Tuileries Garden" caption="Too cold to read, but not too cold to enjoy it." >}}

### Entering the Louvre

I walked to the Louvre and found the line for my time slot. It was very crowded but really well organized, so a big thank you to the staff there.

I had planned a **two-hour visit** focused on what interested me most: **Italian Renaissance paintings, French paintings and Greek sculpture**. I'm interested in a ton of other things there too, but I didn't want to spend the whole day inside. Maybe next time.

We entered through the **Pyramid**. I already had the floor guide on my phone, so I followed my route.

### The Winged Victory

My first masterpiece was the **Winged Victory of Samothrace**. After that I turned right toward the **Denon wing** and started with the Italian paintings.

### The Grande Galerie

The **Grande Galerie** is full of astonishing pieces. Among them I can name Raphael's ***La Belle Jardinière*** and his portrait of ***Castiglione***.

### The Mona Lisa and the Wedding Feast at Cana

Then I walked into the **Salle des États**, the most crowded room in the museum, because that's where *La Joconde*, the **Mona Lisa**, lives.

I was lucky. It wasn't *super* crowded, and I moved easily to the line for a good shot. To be honest, it wasn't the most impressive painting for me, mostly because of the noise and the crowd around it.

I enjoyed the painting on the opposite wall much more: **The Wedding Feast at Cana**. It is an absolutely huge canvas, and it deserves a lot of time.

{{< figure src="louvre-pyramid.jpg" alt="The Louvre Pyramid" caption="The Pyramid, on the way in." >}}

### French paintings

I crossed to the other side of the wing, where the French paintings hang. They took my breath away: **Liberty Leading the People**, **The Raft of the Medusa**, **The Coronation of Napoleon** and many more.

### Greek sculpture

Then I went downstairs to the Greek sculpture: the **Venus de Milo**, the **Borghese Gladiator**, **Michelangelo's Slaves** and others.

Seeing all of them in person was mind-blowing. I rested for a while in the Pyramid hall, then said goodbye to the museum.

### Lunch and rap CDs at Les Halles

The next stop was **Westfield Forum des Halles** for lunch and some shopping.

One thing I wanted to buy during my visit to France was an iconic **rap album**. I spent a good while at **Fnac**, browsing French rap old and new, on CD and on vinyl. Honestly I'd have loved the vinyl, but I thought of what the luggage might do to it on the way back home, so I chose **CDs**. The funny part is that I don't have a player for either format. I'm just buying for fun. :D

### Tour Saint-Jacques and rue de Rivoli

After recharging, I visited the nearby **Tour Saint-Jacques**, then took a long walk along **rue de Rivoli**.

### Golden hour at the Pont Alexandre III

My last landmark of the day was the **Pont Alexandre III**, at golden hour. The yellow lamps and the gilded statues together made it one of the best views of the trip.

### One more hot chocolate

After such a long day I went back to **rue de Buci** for a hot chocolate. I like how lively this street is. Then I took **line 4 and line 11** home.
