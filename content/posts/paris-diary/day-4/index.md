*Paris, 6 to 11 November 2026. This is the last part of my Paris diary. Previous: [Day 3]({{< relref "/posts/paris-diary/day-3" >}}).*

## Day 4: Last Day, Many Stops (Tuesday, 10 November)

### A rainy morning, and a lot to fit in

It was a rainy morning again. This was the last day of my visit and I had many stops to squeeze in.

### Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur

My first destination was **Montmartre and the Sacré-Cœur**. I took **metro line 2**, which goes straight there with no transfer.

I arrived early, around **9 am**, and walked up **rue de Steinkerque**. The souvenir shops were just opening. Near the end of the street the basilica appears on top of the hill, and it is a majestic view.

I took the stairs up and stopped at every level. Each one reveals a different part of Paris, even under a cloudy sky, all the way to the top.

I noticed something there: a **huge number of padlocks** hanging on the fences around the basilica. I wondered whether this was the replacement for the Pont des Arts tradition, after the authorities banned it there. A quick search online confirmed my guess.

I enjoyed the view, then went into the basilica for a short tour.

{{< figure src="sacre-coeur.jpg" alt="Sacré-Cœur basilica on a cloudy morning" caption="Sacré-Cœur from rue de Steinkerque." >}}

### Wandering around Montmartre

Afterwards I strolled around Montmartre, going up and down its many staircases. I visited the **Dalida bust** and **La Maison Rose**, and ended up at the **Place du Tertre**.

I had a typical Parisian breakfast there and rested for a while, enjoying the autumn colors and the old buildings.

### Galeries Lafayette and its rooftop

Next I took the metro to **Galeries Lafayette**. It was super crowded, so I skipped all the floors and went straight up to the **rooftop terrace**. The view is beautiful, and you can see almost all the landmarks from there.

Then I went down one floor to the **gifts and souvenirs** floor. They have beautiful pieces, of better quality than the souvenir shops on the streets, but pricier of course.

### Boulevard Haussmann

I walked a bit along **Boulevard Haussmann**, taking pictures of the Haussmannian and post-Haussmannian buildings. These are what make Paris such a charming city. My phone now holds a lot of photos of grey roofs. :D

### The Marais

The day before, I had booked the last slot I could find for **Notre-Dame**: **16:30**. That left me about three hours, so I had lunch and then made a small discovery of the **Marais**.

I visited the **Place des Vosges**, then got lost in the old streets, looking for the old buildings that remain, like the **Hôtel Hérouet**. I sat on a café terrace for a coffee to recharge.

### Notre-Dame

When the time came I took a quick metro ride of two stops. I was starting to feel the tiredness piling up from the last few days.

I walked past the **Hôtel de Ville**, a beautiful building, crossed the bridge, and saw the marvelous cathedral. There was a lot of crowd, as expected, and a very long queue for people without an online reservation. I skipped it and joined a short one. Always prepare your visits.

After the security check I could already see the details of the facade up close. I have a quick confession to make: **Gothic architecture is my weakness**. Pointed arches, ribbed vaults, large stained glass windows, sculpted portals, gargoyles... I admire the effort and time people put into building such landmarks back then. Each one took generations to finish, a lifetime project.

{{< figure src="notre-dame.jpg" alt="The facade of Notre-Dame de Paris" caption="Notre-Dame, up close." >}}

When I came out, I strolled along the **quai** to see the sides of the cathedral. There was still some restoration work going on after the **2019 fire**.

### One last evening walk

It was already dark, so I had dinner nearby, took one last stroll through the Parisian boulevards, and went back to the hotel.

---

## Final thoughts

That was it. I haven't had enough of Paris, and I already miss it. I still have many places and activities on my list for the next visit.

For a first trip, I think I saw a lot: **10 arrondissements** in total (the 1st, 4th, 5th, 6th, 7th, 8th, 9th, 16th, 18th and 20th), each with its own vibe.

I really recommend visiting Paris. Just plan ahead, especially for places like the Louvre and Notre-Dame. And I hope these diaries can inspire someone's plan.

Thanks for reading, and see you next time.
